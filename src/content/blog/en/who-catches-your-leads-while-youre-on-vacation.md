A lead who reaches out through your website is comparing options at that exact moment. How fast you respond largely decides whether that comparison works in your favor. Research covering more than 250,000 inbound leads across roughly 1,200 companies shows just how large that effect is: responding within 1 minute instead of 2 raises the chance of conversion by 391%. Leads contacted within 5 minutes are 21 times more likely to move to qualification than leads reached after 30 minutes.

## The gap between theory and practice

Despite those numbers, the average (median) response time to an inbound B2B lead sits at 42 hours. Only 7% of companies hit the 5-minute benchmark. 63% of companies never respond to a portion of their leads at all, and 81% of slow responders simply lose that lead to a competitor. That's revenue left on the table every single day: 78% of B2B buyers go with the first vendor to make contact, regardless of whether that vendor later turns out to offer the best price or solution.

## The familiar situation at many small businesses

At businesses still handling leads manually from a shared inbox, 42 hours is more the rule than the exception: especially in a week when someone happens to be sick or on vacation. And that's exactly the problem: vacation periods cluster. If the salesperson who usually responds fast is out for two weeks, and their colleagues are either just back or about to leave themselves, the delay isn't a few hours: it's days. By then the lead has already emailed three other suppliers and stuck with whoever responded first.

## What it costs to leave this alone

The consequence doesn't show up as an obvious error: there's no error message when a lead slips away. It tends to feel like "summer is just quieter," when the real cause is that nobody responded within a few minutes. That's a silent revenue leak: leads that were genuinely there, but went to a competitor who simply happened to be faster, often unnoticed until the pipeline turns out surprisingly empty come September.

Test it yourself: send a test message through your own contact form this week and time how long it takes to get a reply: not an automatic confirmation, but an actual first response.
