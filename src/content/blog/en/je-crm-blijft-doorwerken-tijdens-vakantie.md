It's the familiar reflex right before you leave: one more quick check of email, one more login "just in case." Research by Dutch bank Knab among more than 2,000 self-employed professionals shows this isn't an isolated slip-up: it's the norm. While 63.8% of self-employed workers take four weeks of vacation or more, only 32% are actually fully offline during that time. 36% deliberately stay in touch: sending an invoice, calling a client back, quickly scanning email.

## Why letting go is so hard

Of those who keep working, 60% say they mostly enjoy it: it's simply part of the deal. But the numbers also paint a less carefree picture: 40% feel financial pressure around vacation time, and 42% take less time off than they'd actually like. That's telling, because the same data shows a clear correlation: entrepreneurs who take six weeks or more of vacation rate self-employment an average of 8.5. Those who take little vacation don't get past a 7.6.

## The familiar situation at many small businesses

At businesses still running on Excel, Word, and a shared Outlook inbox, that urge to keep checking usually isn't workaholism: it's a very practical problem: if you don't look, nobody does. A quote that needs following up exists only in your head and your inbox. A lead coming in through the contact form lands with one person, and that person is at the pool. Without a system around it, "letting go for a bit" is the same as "doing nothing about the business for a bit": and for many entrepreneurs, that feels irresponsible.

## What it costs if you don't fix this

That tension between working and not working is exactly where vacation loses its value. You're physically there, but with one eye on your phone. That's not rest, and it's not good customer follow-up either: a half-checked email tends to produce a late or half-baked reply more often than a deliberately set up automatic response does. In the long run, it's also a team problem: if the business is structurally dependent on one person who has to stay reachable at all times, that's not a business: it's a job with no days off.

## How FortCRM helps with this

At FortCRM, we set up Pipedrive so that most of the follow-up doesn't have to live in one person's head. Through automation, we build rules: an incoming lead gets categorized and receives a first response immediately, a quote that's been sitting for a week automatically triggers a reminder, and any exception that genuinely needs a human generates a clear notification for a colleague: not for you, on the beach. That shifts "I have to keep checking" into "the system will let someone know if it matters," and that's a fundamentally different kind of vacation.

## One concrete first step

Heading out soon? Before you leave, set up at least one automatic rule: a task that gets reassigned to someone else if a deal hasn't been followed up on in a week, or an automatic reply that points to a colleague for anything urgent. That's not a big implementation, but it is the difference between constantly checking your phone and actually being gone for two weeks.
